01 / The perspective

What makes a behavior anomalous?

Anomaly detection identifies behavior patterns that do not conform to expected behavior. This survey organizes methods by how they establish and apply the decision conditions.

108 examples

Boundary-Modeling AD

Constructs a boundary from the statistical, structural, or feature regularities of historical runtime data. Detection checks whether current behavior patterns lie within the accepted region.

Alignment-Based AD

Constructs an alignment model connecting current behavior patterns with applicable requirements under the execution context. Detection evaluates its decision conditions; requirement violations indicate anomalies.

The central difficulty

Different behavior patterns may fulfill the same request. Similar output formats or execution structures may still conceal anomalies. Historical resemblance alone may therefore be insufficient for a reliable anomaly decision.

Five open challenges

01Uncertainty in Requirements

Ambiguous or implicit goals can lead to different anomaly decisions for the same behavior. Requirement instantiation must preserve intended goals and constraints while making unresolved ambiguity explicit.

02Multi-Requirement Alignment

SLO, user intent, and safety requirements can interact or conflict. Evaluation must retain separate condition-level results and evidence; achieving one goal does not cancel a mandatory constraint violation.

03Distortion in Alignment

Mapping requirements and behavior into related representations may weaken features that reveal a violation. Representational similarity should not be mistaken for conformity to expected behavior.

04Overhead in Alignment

Evidence retrieval, inference, verification, and model updates incur runtime costs. Incremental updates and selective verification must be evaluated alongside detection effectiveness and delay.

05Interference from Intervention

Blocking, retrying, or replanning too early may interrupt useful reasoning and self-correction. Intervention should account for subsequent execution and final task outcomes.
